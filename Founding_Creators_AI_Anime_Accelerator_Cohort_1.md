## Core Program Concept

**Program Type:** Live, interactive, in-person youth creative technology
accelerator\
**Length:** 6 weeks\
**Cohort:** Cohort One / Inaugural Founding Creators\
**Founding Creator Tuition:** \$1,597\
**Regular Tuition:** \$2,097

### Core Promise

> **Create something you never thought you could: the beginning of your
> own original anime universe.**

Students are not simply taking an anime class. They enter a creative
studio experience where they learn to develop original characters,
stories, worlds, scenes, and portfolio pieces using modern AI-assisted
creative tools.

# 1. The Big Vision

The six-week accelerator is the entry point into a larger youth
creative-tech pathway:

**Accelerator → Character Reveal & Creator Showcase → Founding Creator
Alumni → Portfolio Development → Peer Leadership → Paid Opportunities →

**"I didn't know I could do this."**

# 2. Who the Program Is For

The program is designed to expand representation and participation in
anime, animation, AI-assisted creativity, and digital storytelling.

The program should be welcoming to young creators who want to build
original characters and worlds while intentionally creating space for
young people who have historically seen less representation in these
creative fields.

Avoid making race-based promises or scholarship eligibility requirements
without appropriate legal review, especially when outside funding or
sponsorship is involved.

# 5. Suggested Six-Week Curriculum

## Week 1 --- Become the Creator

**Goal:** Introduce students to the studio, tools, creative process, and
original character development.

Students: - Enter the Founding Creator studio experience - Learn the
difference between inspiration and copying - Explore original anime
character concepts - Choose character traits - Begin character design -
Create their first character draft - Save their Day 1 work for the
transformation portfolio

**Progress Capture:** Day 1 interview + first character image.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Week 2 --- Build Your Character

**Goal:** Develop a consistent, recognizable original character.

Students explore: - Face - Hair - Skin tone - Eyes - Clothing -
Accessories - Color palette - Personality - Expressions - Character
consistency

**Portfolio Piece:** Polished character reference.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Week 3 --- Give Your Character a Story

**Goal:** Move from a picture to a character with purpose.

Students develop: - Name - Personality - Strengths - Challenges -
Goals - Relationships - Backstory - Story conflict - Supporting
characters

**Portfolio Piece:** Character profile + story concept.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Week 4 --- Design Your Anime World

**Goal:** Build the environment where the character's story takes place.

Students create: - World concept - City, school, futuristic environment,
fantasy location, or other setting - Important locations - Environment
colors and visual identity - Scenes showing the character inside the
world

**Portfolio Piece:** World-building board + cinematic scene.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Week 5 --- Bring the Universe to Life

**Goal:** Introduce motion, scenes, storytelling, and animation.

Students: - Plan a short sequence - Create scene prompts - Explore
camera angles - Explore movement - Add appropriate dialogue or
narration - Create a short animated sequence or trailer

**Portfolio Piece:** Short animation/trailer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Week 6 --- Creator Studio & Showcase Preparation

**Goal:** Polish the work and prepare students to present as creators.

Students: - Review their Day 1 work - Polish final character - Select
best scenes - Complete portfolio - Practice explaining their creative
choices - Prepare Character Reveal presentation - Record final
reflection

**Portfolio Piece:** Cohort One Creator Portfolio.

# 9. Simple Time-Lapse Content System

Do not try to film every student continuously.

Use:

**Screen Recording + Short Classroom Clip + Final Reveal**

### Process

1.  Student starts screen recording before a major creation activity.
2.  Student works normally for 20--45 minutes.
3.  Stop the recording.
4.  Speed the recording up later in a video editor.
5.  Capture 5--10 seconds of the student physically working.
6.  Finish with the completed character or scene.

Possible 30-second video:

-   0--3 sec: Student working
-   3--18 sec: Fast screen-recorded creation process
-   18--23 sec: Finished character
-   23--30 sec: Student reaction or final reveal

Feature only a few students each week while still saving everyone's
portfolio milestones.

All recording, promotional use, testimonials, and display of student
work should follow appropriate parent/guardian permissions.

# 26. Program Character --- Aoi Freeman

The program has an official original character who serves as the **Studio
Guide**: the on-screen host for all marketing, social content, and studio
signage.

## Who she is

**Name:** Aoi Freeman
**Role:** Studio Guide, Founding Creators studio
**Type:** Original character owned by the program. Not derived from any
existing anime or franchise.
**Reads as:** an adult mentor in her early twenties, not a student.

Aoi is the professional in the room --- the one who already has the portfolio
and is turning around to hand the process to someone coming up behind her. She
teaches; she does not sell. She uses the real craft vocabulary and then defines
it, because respecting a young creator means giving them the actual word.

## Why she exists

Many young Black creators grow up loving anime without ever seeing a character
built from their own features, and without meeting anyone who tells them that
character could be theirs to build. Aoi is the answer standing in the frame: an
anime character with deep brown skin and natural coily hair, designed with the
same detail and care the medium gives anyone else.

She also models the program's originality principle. She is proof that
"be inspired, don't copy, build your own universe" produces something worth
looking at.

## Locked design summary

Full specification, palette, generation prompt, and QC checklist:
**`content/AOI-FREEMAN-CHARACTER-BIBLE.md`**

- **Skin:** rich deep brown, warm undertones. Never lightened.
- **Hair:** very long, voluminous natural coily-curly hair with defined curl
  clumps, indigo-violet with glowing lilac fiber-optic strands. Heart hair pins.
- **Eyes:** amethyst violet with lilac catchlights.
- **Outer layer:** oversized violet patent moto jacket, popped collar, silver
  zippers, heart studs, glowing tech light-bar on the left sleeve, long coat
  tail.
- **Top:** high-neck black structured bodice, fully covered to the collarbone.
- **Waist:** wide layered black belt with silver heart buckles, silver hip chain
  with a glowing violet heart charm.
- **Skirt:** layered violet tulle high-low ruffled skirt with a long back panel.
- **Legs:** **opaque black full-length leggings.** Locked and non-negotiable.
- **Thigh hardware:** black straps with silver heart rings and a heart-emblem
  utility pouch, worn over the leggings.
- **Boots:** violet knee-high lace-up patent boots, silver eyelets, low block
  heel.
- **Motif:** the heart, repeated throughout. The visual signature of the studio.
- **Setting:** neon futuristic city rooftop at dusk, violet and magenta towers,
  coral and pink sunset sky.

## Design standards --- non-negotiable

Aoi appears in youth education marketing, so her presentation is held to a
standard and it does not flex for a single render.

1. **Full coverage.** Opaque black leggings, high closed neckline, no bare
   midriff, no sheer fabric, no exposed thigh. The leggings were a deliberate
   revision to the original design and they are permanent.
2. **No suggestive posing, framing, or camera angles.** She stands, walks, sits,
   teaches, and gestures. Nothing else.
3. **Skin tone is never lightened.** If a render returns lighter than the
   reference, it is a failed render and gets discarded.
4. **Hair texture is never straightened or smoothed.** The coil definition is
   the point of the character.
5. **No romance, flirtation, or innuendo** in any script, caption, or scene.
6. **Dignified and mature at all times.** She is a teacher figure in an
   educational program and every frame should be usable in a parent
   information packet.

Every render passes the seven-point QC checklist in the character bible before
it ships. Seven for seven or it does not post.

# 27. Student-Facing Six-Week Curriculum

Section 5 is the internal planning curriculum. **This is the version used with
students, parents, and on the enrollment page.** Where the two differ, this
section governs public-facing material.

**Program promise:** Create something you never thought you could: the beginning
of your own original anime universe.

Students enter a creative animation studio where they develop an original
character, story, world, and short anime scene using AI-supported creative
tools.

## Week 1 --- Enter the Studio

**Theme:** Discover Your Creator Identity

Students will:

-   Receive their Founding Creator Cohort 01 badge
-   Explore different anime styles and story genres
-   Choose the main idea for their anime universe
-   Create a Creator Vision Board

**Take-home creation:** Anime Universe Idea + Creator Vision Board

## Week 2 --- Create Your Main Character

**Theme:** Bring Your Hero to Life

Students will choose:

-   Character name and age
-   Skin tone, facial features, eyes, and hairstyle
-   Clothing, colors, accessories, and technology
-   Personality, strengths, challenges, and special skills

**Take-home creation:** Original Main Character Profile + Character Image

## Week 3 --- Build the Anime World

**Theme:** Where Does Your Story Happen?

Students will create:

-   The name of their anime world
-   The location and time period
-   Important buildings, technology, and environments
-   The rules of their universe

**Take-home creation:** Anime World Description + World Background Image

## Week 4 --- Write the Story

**Theme:** Give Your Character a Mission

Students will decide:

-   What their character wants
-   What problem the character must solve
-   Who helps the character
-   What stands in the character's way
-   How the story begins

**Take-home creation:** Original Story Summary + Simple Six-Scene Storyboard

## Week 5 --- Create the Anime Scene

**Theme:** Turn the Story Into Motion

Students will:

-   Select one storyboard scene
-   Write the action, camera movement, and dialogue
-   Create images for the scene
-   Use an AI animation tool to add movement
-   Add sound, music, or the character's voice

**Take-home creation:** Short Original Anime Scene

## Week 6 --- Prepare for the Character Reveal

**Theme:** Present Your Universe

Students will:

-   Improve their character, world, or animated scene
-   Organize their work into a beginner portfolio
-   Practice introducing their character
-   Prepare their Creator Showcase presentation

**Final presentation:** Each student introduces their character, explains their
anime universe, and presents their finished creation.

## Character Reveal & Creator Showcase

Families and invited guests attend the final showcase.

Each Founding Creator receives:

-   A Founding Creator Cohort 01 certificate
-   An individualized achievement award
-   A printed or digital creator portfolio
-   Recognition during the Character Reveal
-   Founding Creator alumni status

Possible awards include: Character Design, Storytelling, World Building, Visual
Innovation, Creative Courage, Problem Solving, Technical Exploration, Detail and
Design, Creative Growth, Studio Leadership, Collaboration, Most Improved.

## What Every Student Completes

By the end of six weeks, every student will have:

1.  An original anime character
2.  A character profile
3.  An original anime world
4.  A story summary
5.  A six-scene storyboard
6.  A short animated scene
7.  A beginner creative portfolio
8.  A Character Reveal presentation

## Simple parent-facing description

> During this six-week studio experience, young creators develop the beginning
> of an original anime universe. They design a character, build a world, write a
> story, create a storyboard, and produce a short animated scene. Cohort One
> concludes with a Character Reveal and Creator Showcase for family and friends.

### Note on the two curricula

Section 5 and Section 27 describe the same six weeks with a different ordering
of world-building and story. Section 5 places story in Week 3 and world in Week
4; Section 27 reverses them. **Section 27 is what students and parents see.**
Pick one order before Week 1 and align the internal plan to it so weekly
deliverables and marketing don't contradict each other.
