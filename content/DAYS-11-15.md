## Instagram caption

For the parents reading this.

It's reasonable to look at an AI anime program and think it's kids playing on
computers. Here's what the six weeks actually put a young person through.

**They make decisions and defend them.** Why this silhouette. Why these three
colors and not five. Why does this character want the thing they want. Every
week we ask them to explain a choice, and "it looks cool" isn't accepted.

**They structure a story.** Goal, obstacle, ally, opposition, opening. That's
narrative architecture, and it's the same skill as an essay.

**They finish on a deadline.** Six weeks, weekly deliverables, a fixed showcase
date. A lot of creative kids have never taken something all the way to done.

**They evaluate a tool's output.** They learn what good looks like first, then
judge what the AI hands back and decide whether to keep it. That's real AI
literacy, and it holds up regardless of what tools exist in ten years.

**They stand up and present.** In front of family and guests, they explain their
character, their world, and their choices. For some of them it's the first time
they've spoken about their own work in public.

Judgment. Structure. Follow-through. Technical fluency. Public speaking.

Anime is the doorway. What's on the other side of it is the point.

## After Day 15

Don't let the account go quiet the moment enrollment closes. Two options that
keep it alive with almost no new production:

**If Cohort One filled:** switch to build-up content. Aoi previews what week one
looks like, the studio gets set up on camera, badges arrive. Then document the
cohort itself — the time-lapse system in the program document turns each week
into a post, and real student transformation outperforms anything written in
advance.

**If seats remain:** re-run Days 3, 8, and 10 with fresh hooks. Those three
carry the strongest arguments — the hair lesson, the career map, and the "you
don't have to draw" objection — and the audience that arrived late never saw
them.
