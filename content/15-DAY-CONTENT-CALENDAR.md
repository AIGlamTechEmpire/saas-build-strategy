(Six-Week Program)

---

## The strategy in one paragraph

Fifteen days, and the first ten of them barely mention the program. Aoi teaches
first — the history of Black anime fandom, why the representation gap exists,
what the actual craft vocabulary is, what AI does and does not do. By the time
enrollment shows up on Day 12, the audience has had ten days of proof that this
brand knows what it's talking about. Parents don't buy a class from a stranger.
They buy it from the person who already taught their kid something for free.

**Ratio:** 10 educational days, 3 bridge days, 2 enrollment days.

## Posting rhythm

**Best windows** for a parent-and-teen audience: weekdays 6–8pm, weekends
10am–1pm. Post the carousel to Instagram first, mirror to Facebook within the
hour, and put the TikTok scene up the same evening.

**Three days a week** across five weeks is a comfortable pace. Five days a week
runs the whole calendar in three weeks and hits harder if enrollment has a
deadline. Pick one and hold it — consistency beats volume.

**Facebook adjustment:** the audience skews toward the parents rather than the
kids. Every day below includes a rewritten Facebook caption that leads with the
outcome for the child instead of the anime hook.

## The one compliance note

Days 2, 3, and 8 talk directly about race and representation, and they should —
it's the honest reason this program exists. Keep the language exactly as
written: the program is **built to widen participation and is open to every
young creator**. Do not add eligibility language, scholarship promises, or
selection criteria based on race in any caption or ad. That's the line the
program document already draws, and paid promotion is where platforms enforce
it hardest.
