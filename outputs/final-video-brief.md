## The Mechanism

# THE REVENUE BUILD ORDER

The sequence is not the mechanism. **The enforcement is.**

"Research, then offer, then content, then follow-up" is the standard direct-response sequence, and this audience has heard it. Naming it does not make it proprietary. What makes it a system rather than advice is that it is a **dependency graph with pass conditions**: a downstream seat is structurally unable to start, because the file it needs does not exist yet.

Three components. All three visible on disk.

### 1. The Order — artifact-gated

```
  SIGNAL ──▶ OFFER ──▶ ANGLE ──▶ CONVERSION
     │  gate   │  gate   │  gate  │
     └────────┴────────┴───────┘
        one rejection permitted per gate
```

| Seat | Emits | Downstream cannot start without it |
|---|---|---|
| SIGNAL | `01-signal.md` — a scored buyer plus a disqualification list | OFFER has nobody to price for |
| OFFER | `02-offer.md` — one named offer, one defended number | ANGLE has nothing to make people want |
| ANGLE | `03-angles.md` — three angles, each tied to a line in the offer | CONVERSION has no promise to follow up on |
| CONVERSION | `04-conversion.md` — lead magnet plus sequence | — |

Seats read **files**, not conversation. No artifact, no run.

### 2. The Gate — the Challenge Pass

**No seat is permitted to be agreeable.** Before any artifact moves downstream, the receiving seat scores it against a published rubric and may reject it **once**, in writing, with the reason. Five criteria, 1–5 each. **Any criterion below 3 is an automatic rejection.** The thresholds ship in the Kit; they are not vibes.

The OFFER seat's rubric on an incoming signal:

| Criterion | Fails at | Failure looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer is nameable | <3 | "small business owners" |
| Buyer has a dated event | <3 | no deadline, no trigger, no calendar |
| Buyer is disqualified from something | <3 | the seat could not name who this is *not* for |
| Pain is priced elsewhere already | <3 | no comparable spend exists |
| Input sufficiency | <3 | brief fields are empty — reject on the fields, **by name** |

That last row is load-bearing. A seat must reject on **insufficient input**, not only on bad output. Otherwise three vague lines get a vague pass and the mechanism silently no-ops.

**Hard rule: one rejection per handoff, in the Kit exactly as on camera.** v1's friction check built a sales trigger on a *second* rejection pointing at the paid workshop. That contradicts the mechanism's own stated rule, and to an audience defined by having been sold to too many times it reads as a paywall wearing a rubric's clothes. If a second pass still falls short, the agent **accepts it with a named soft flag** — *"This will run, but it's still soft on [field]. That's the part a tuned rubric would catch."* Honest, non-blocking, and a better paid-tier trigger than a locked door.

### 3. The Override — the named human calibration point

Every seat has exactly one decision it is **forbidden** to finalize. OFFER can recommend a price; it cannot set one. ANGLE can rank angles; it cannot pick the one you film. The override is a required field in the artifact and must carry written reasoning.

On camera: the agent recommends $600. PinkyF writes $450, and the *why* goes in the file.

### 4. The Broken-Order Test — the proof

**New in this version, and the most important addition.** At 9:59, run Seat 3 first — no offer file, same business, same model, same day — and put the two outputs side by side on one screen.

| Undefined run | Ordered run |
|---|---|
| "5 Bookkeeping Tips For Small Business Owners" | "The IFTA Deadline Is In 11 Days And Your Fuel Receipts Are In A Shoebox" |

> "Same tool. Same day. Same business. One of them had a defined offer to stand on."

Everyone in this niche *asserts* that order matters. This shows the delta. It converts doctrine into an experiment, and it is the reason a skeptic stops arguing.

> **One prompt gives you an answer. A build order gives you an asset you can be argued with about, and still win.**

**Why a single prompt cannot copy this:** it has no second party, no artifact boundary, no pass threshold, and no place to record a decision it was not allowed to make. You can copy the four words. You cannot copy the disagreement.

At $297 × 12 it is worth running and worth showing up prepared for, and it does what a workshop is actually good at: twelve qualified buyers in a room with you for two hours immediately before you name the Sprint. Install Day's job is qualification and proximity, not margin. Includes the Calibration Pack.

**Rung 5 — The Sprint. Six countable artifacts, not "calibration":**

1. Offer page **published at a live URL**
2. Lead magnet built and **delivered by a working email**
3. Five-email sequence **installed and sending**
4. Three content angles scripted to hook and outline
5. Their agent folder, tuned to their category, theirs to keep
6. **The price quoted to at least one real prospect before Day 14**

Item 6 is the completion criterion. The Sprint is not done when the assets exist. It is done when the number has been said out loud to a stranger.

**Risk reversal:** *"If your offer isn't live at a URL on Day 14, the next 14 days are free."* A completion guarantee, not a refund — cheaper, more honest, and it selects for people who intend to ship.

**Rung 7 — Agent Ops, defined or don't sell it.** Monthly: one new seat file, one rubric re-tuned against the buyer's last 30 days of real results, and a quarterly full re-run with a written diff. Undefined subscriptions churn at month two.

### Kill this line

> ~~"The machine is free. Aiming it is the paid part."~~

It sounds excellent and it sells nothing. "Aiming" is a service-quality adjective — a buyer cannot picture it, compare it, or tell afterward whether they got it. Worse, it concedes that the machine you spent fourteen minutes making desirable is worth zero.

**Replace with:** *"The Kit gets you a defined offer. The Sprint gets you a live one. Those are different objects, and only one of them has ever produced a payment."*

The gap is **defined vs. shipped**, not *machine vs. aim*.

## Open Decisions

Six calls the agent team cannot make for you.

1. **Confirm every price.** $97 / $297 / $1,497 / 2×$799 / $7,500 / $297-mo are recommendations. Once final, **strip the hedging language** — hedged pricing inside a close reads as uncertainty and costs conversions.
2. **Sprint: checkout or application?** $1,497 with direct checkout works from cold YouTube. Above that a human has to talk to them, which means ~$2,997 with an application. **Pick one; do not straddle.**
3. **Real business or disclosed composite?** A real named business beats Ledger & Lane. Without one, say on camera: *"This is a composite of three real bookkeepers I've talked to, and the numbers are real market numbers."* A skeptical audience forgives a disclosed composite; it does not forgive a hypothetical dressed as real.
4. **Is Install Day on the calendar with a date, time, and time zone?** If not, cut every deadline reference from Emails 5 and 6.
5. **Five emails or six?** The sixth (Day 12 re-open) exists because activation lags the calendar. Dropping it returns you to the five you asked for, with most of the gain intact.
6. **Reply-volume cutoff for Email 1.** At what download count does the manual reply become a two-field form?

### One number to verify before shooting

**$450/month for a single-truck owner-operator's bookkeeping is plausible and near the upper end** — comfortable for a 2-to-5-truck fleet, defensible as a composite, but **not evidenced**. This build had no web access; the figure is labeled inference throughout.

If you can get one real quote from one real trucking bookkeeper before filming, do. If you can't, **drop the retainer to $400.** It costs $600/year of headline and buys total defensibility — and defensibility is the entire product.
