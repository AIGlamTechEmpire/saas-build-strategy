# Interview Bank

Use this in Phase 3 of the workflow. Read the whole file before starting the interview.

## How to use this bank

After Phase 2 scraping, you have a confidence map of what you extracted vs. what's missing. Use that map to drive which clusters to ask, in what depth.

For each cluster:
1. Read the cluster's intro to understand what the questions are after.
2. Show the user what you already extracted for that cluster.
3. Ask the priority questions (marked 🥇). Skip lower-priority questions if you already have enough.
4. Stop when you have what you need. Don't keep asking just because there are more questions on the list.

**Hard cap: 15 total questions across the entire interview.** If you find yourself needing more, the schemas have more `[TO REFINE]` markers than you'd like, but that's better than burning out the user.

## Interview style

- **Batch related questions together.** Ask 2-4 at a time.
- **Use the Talk to a Friend reframe.** When the user freezes, prompt: *"If you had to explain this to your closest friend in your industry, what would you say?"*
- **Tell the user it's okay to be rough.** Their words become drafts you polish later.
- **Acknowledge before moving on.** Brief affirmations ("Got it." "That's useful.") keep the conversation flowing without padding.
- **Never use the questions as written verbatim.** Adapt the wording to match the conversation. The bank is a guide, not a script.

## Cluster A: Identity Gaps

**What this cluster fills:** Company DNA sections that the website didn't fully capture — usually the emotional layer of the origin story, the true mission, the personality.

**Skip this cluster if:** The About page already has a strong origin story, mission, and clear values.

### Priority questions

🥇 **Origin story emotional layer.** "I have the basic origin story from your About page. What I'm missing is the EMOTIONAL part. Why did YOU start this, personally? What was the moment you decided? What were you angry about, or what did you want to fix?"

🥇 **The real mission.** "Beyond making money, why does this business exist? If you had to write a one-sentence mission you'd actually believe, not the corporate version, what would it be?"

🥈 **Personality in 3 words.** "Pick 3 adjectives that describe your business's personality. Not aspirational. Actual. If I were a friend describing your brand to another friend at a coffee shop, what would I say?"

🥈 **Differentiation in plain words.** "What do you do differently from everyone else in your space? Not the polished version, the truthful version. What's the edge that makes you, you?"

🥉 **Values you live by.** "What are the 3 to 5 values your business actually operates by, not the ones on a wall? The ones you'd fire someone over if they violated."

## Cluster B: ICP Psychology

**What this cluster fills:** The deep psychology of the ICP — pain, fear, beliefs, vocabulary. Almost always missing from public materials.

**Skip this cluster if:** The user has a rich ICP-focused sales page that already names pain, fears, and beliefs.

### Priority questions

🥇 **The emotional state when they find you.** "Imagine your ideal customer in the moment they're about to find your business. What's their emotional state? Frustrated? Burnt out? Desperate? Optimistic? What's the moment that made them search?"

🥇 **What they currently believe that's wrong.** "What does your ICP currently believe about your category that is WRONG? What do they need to un-believe before they can buy from you?"

🥈 **Where they've been burned.** "Where have they tried this before and gotten burned? What past experience makes them skeptical of someone like you?"

🥈 **Their secret desire.** "What does success look like to them — not the surface version, the secret one? The version they wouldn't say on LinkedIn?"

🥉 **Vocabulary patterns.** "What 3 to 5 words or phrases does your ICP use that nobody else does? And what words do they ROLL THEIR EYES at? Words that make them think someone's a phony?"

## Cluster C: Offer Stack Details

**What this cluster fills:** Pricing structure (if not public), ascension path, hero customer stories.

**Skip this cluster if:** The website has clear pricing for every offer, and case study pages have hero customer narratives.

### Priority questions

🥇 **Ascension path.** "Walk me through your customer journey. Where does someone typically START with you, and where do they end up? What's the natural progression of offers?"

🥇 **Pricing for high-ticket offers.** (Only ask if pricing wasn't on the website.) "What's the price structure for your main offer? Is it one-time, monthly, annual? What range of price points should the AI know about?"

🥈 **Hero customer story.** "Tell me about ONE customer who got transformative results from working with you. Who were they before, what changed, what are they doing now? Use their actual words if you can remember them."

🥈 **Common objection killers.** "What are the top 2-3 objections you hear from prospects, and how do you typically respond? The actual response you'd give on a sales call."

## Cluster D: Brand Voice Refinement

**What this cluster fills:** The voice nuances that mining writing can't capture — banned words, AI tells they hate, voice variation by context.

**Skip this cluster if:** You scraped 10+ writing samples and the voice is consistent across all of them.

### Priority questions

🥇 **Voice samples verification.** "I extracted these 3-5 writing samples that I think represent your voice [paste samples]. Do these feel right? Anything missing? Anything you'd never write today?"

🥇 **Banned words and phrases.** "What words or phrases do you NEVER want to see in your AI's output? Corporate clichés, AI tells, words that feel inauthentic to you. The more specific the better."

🥈 **AI tells you hate.** "Which AI writing patterns make you cringe? Examples: 'It's not X, it's Y' constructions, 'Let's dive in,' three-part lists with rhyming endings, rhetorical questions in series, 'game-changing,' 'revolutionary,' em-dashes."

🥉 **Voice variation by context.** "Does your voice change between sales copy and casual social posts? If yes, briefly describe the variation."

## Cluster E: Operating Principles

**What this cluster fills:** Almost the entire Operating Principles doc. This is the hardest doc to extract from public materials.

**Skip this cluster ONLY if:** You miraculously found a public "How We Work" page that covered most of this. (Rare.)

### Priority questions

🥇 **Decision-making lens.** "When you're stuck on a hard call in your business, what's the lens you default to? What question do you ask yourself, or what principle do you fall back on?"

🥇 **Quality bar.** "What's your quality bar for shipping work? When is something 'good enough' to send? Perfectionism is too slow, but what's your standard?"

🥈 **Communication standards.** "What are your communication norms? Response time expectations? What channels do you actually use for what? Email vs. Slack vs. text?"

🥈 **What you say YES to / NO to.** "What do you say NO to that others in your space say YES to? That's often where your edge is. What do you say YES to that surprises people?"

🥉 **Business rhythms.** "What are your weekly and monthly rhythms? What happens every week without fail? What happens every month? These become the standards an AI should know about."

🥉 **Customer service philosophy.** "How do you handle a customer who's unhappy? What's your default move? What would you NEVER do?"

## Cluster F: 4Ds Mapping (Work Ownership)

**What this cluster fills:** The Work Ownership Map section of the Operating Principles doc. Where each function in the business currently sits across the 4Ds and where it's heading.

**Skip this cluster if:** The user said in Phase 1 they haven't completed their Org Map yet. Mark the section `[TO REFINE]` and move on.

### Priority questions

🥇 **Current state per function.** "Walk me through your Org Map. For each function (Marketing, Sales, Product, Customer Success, Operations, Finance), where does it sit TODAY on the 4Ds?"
- Doing = You execute the work yourself
- Deciding = Someone executes but every instance needs your judgment
- Delegating = A team member or AI employee owns it
- Designing = You're out of the work, architecting the system

Don't make them answer all 6 functions formally. Just go down the list and ask them to call out the mode for each. 2-3 minutes.

🥇 **Target state per function.** "For each function, where do you WANT it to be in 12 weeks?" Most will say Delegating for everything. That's correct for repetitive operational work. The exception is strategic functions where the answer is Designing (where the founder stays as architect permanently).

🥈 **Functions where you stay at Designing permanently.** "Are there any functions you want to OWN permanently in the Designing mode? Where you architect but never execute? Usually: brand voice approval, hiring senior team, big partnerships, strategic direction."

🥉 **Priority order for the 12-week build.** "If you could move just ONE function up the ladder first, which one? What's bleeding the most right now?" This often differs from the AWA curriculum order. Capture it so the AI knows the user's strategic priority even if the curriculum says otherwise.

## Closing the interview

When you have what you need, signal the end clearly:

> *"That's everything I need. I'm going to draft your six Brain documents now. This takes me a few minutes. I'll save them to your outputs folder and walk you through each one when I'm done."*

Then move to Phase 4.

## Pre-Launch Mode (no website)

If Phase 1 determined the user is pre-launch, run this expanded interview INSTEAD of the standard clusters. The full Brain has to come from conversation rather than scraping, so plan for 30 to 60 minutes and 25 to 35 questions total.

Sequence the conversation in this order (it builds momentum from concrete to abstract):

### Block 1: The Business Idea (5 questions, fast)

1. *"In one sentence: what does this business do?"*
2. *"In one sentence: who is it for?"*
3. *"What problem does it solve that nobody else is solving well right now?"*
4. *"Is this a service, a product, software, content, or some mix? Walk me through the actual thing customers pay for."*
5. *"What stage are you in? Idea, building, beta with early users, or 'about to launch'?"*

### Block 2: The Founder Story (4 questions)

6. *"Why YOU? What's your background that makes you the right person to build this?"*
7. *"What were you doing right before this that led you here? Walk me through the moment you decided to do this instead."*
8. *"What were you angry about, or what did you see that was broken?"*
9. *"What does success look like in 3 years? In 10?"*

### Block 3: The ICP (6 questions, the deepest dig)

10. *"Picture your ideal first customer. Who are they? Title, industry, life stage, anything specific."*
11. *"How would you describe their emotional state in the moment they'd find your business? Frustrated? Curious? Desperate?"*
12. *"What's the EXACT problem they're trying to solve? Use their words if you can."*
13. *"What have they tried before that didn't work?"*
14. *"What do they currently believe about your category that's WRONG? What do they need to un-believe before they could buy from you?"*
15. *"What does success look like to them? Not the surface version, the secret one."*

### Block 4: The Offer (5 questions)

16. *"What's the FIRST offer you're going to sell? Name, price, what's included."*
17. *"What's the transformation that offer delivers in one sentence?"*
18. *"Who is this first offer FOR specifically? Same as the broad ICP, or a subset?"*
19. *"Do you have a longer-term offer or ascension path in mind? If yes, what are the next 1-2 offers in the journey?"*
20. *"Any offers you're explicitly NOT building? Things you've decided not to do?"*

### Block 5: The Voice (4 questions)

21. *"Three adjectives that describe how this business should SOUND. Not aspirational. The actual personality."*
22. *"If you had to write a sales page for this business right now, what 3-5 sentences capture how you'd want it to open?"*
23. *"What words or phrases do you NEVER want to see in your marketing? Corporate clichés, AI tells, anything that makes you cringe."*
24. *"Are there any writers, brands, or creators whose voice you admire and want to learn from? Name 2-3."*

### Block 6: Proof (3 questions, lighter because they likely have less proof pre-launch)

25. *"Do you have ANY early users, beta testers, or people who've gotten value from a related thing you've done? Names, what they got from it."*
26. *"Do you have credibility from a prior career, a podcast appearance, a book, a degree, anything? List the credentials worth knowing."*
27. *"What's your origin-story proof point? The thing in your own life that proves you can solve this problem?"*

### Block 7: Operating Principles (3 questions)

28. *"When you're stuck on a hard call in this business, what's the lens you want to default to? Customer-first? Speed? Quality? Something else?"*
29. *"What's your quality bar going to be for shipping work? Production-ready, or rough is fine if it ships?"*
30. *"What do you explicitly NOT want to do that others in your space do?"*

### Block 8: Optional deeper questions if the user has energy (5 questions)

31. *"What rhythms do you want to set? Weekly cadences, monthly reviews, anything you want to commit to from day one?"*
32. *"How will you handle an unhappy customer? Default move?"*
33. *"Are you hiring or solo for the foreseeable future?"*
34. *"What's your pricing philosophy? Value-based, market-rate, premium, accessible?"*
35. *"Is there anything important about this business that I haven't asked you about?"*

### Closing the pre-launch interview

> *"That's everything. I'm going to draft your six Brain documents now plus a launch-ready strawman of your messaging. This takes me a few minutes. The Brain we build now becomes the seed of your website, your sales page, and every email you ever send. Nothing here is wasted work."*

Then move to Phase 4.
