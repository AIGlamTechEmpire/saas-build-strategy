# ICP Profile — Westwood Marketing

> Who we serve. How they think. What they need. Every piece of marketing, sales, and customer-facing content this business produces should be written FOR this person.

---

## Demographics

**🟢 Primary ICP description (1-2 sentences):**
A founder or co-founder of a direct-to-consumer e-commerce brand doing $1M to $20M in annual revenue. Usually 3-7 years into the business, post-product-market-fit, currently stuck at a growth plateau they can't break through with their existing approach to paid and email.

**🟢 Demographic specifics:**
- **Title or role:** Founder, co-founder, CEO. Sometimes a head of growth reporting directly to the founder.
- **Industry:** DTC consumer products. Most common categories: skincare/beauty, apparel, food and beverage, supplements, home goods, pet products.
- **Stage:** Past product-market fit. Have customers, have revenue. Have NOT yet figured out how to scale acquisition profitably.
- **Revenue range:** $1M to $20M annually. Sweet spot is $3M to $10M.
- **Team size:** 3 to 20 employees. Often the founder is still doing more operational work than they want.
- **Geography:** Primarily US-based, with some Canadian and Australian brands.
- **Other identifiers:** They have a Shopify or Shopify Plus store. They use Klaviyo for email. They have a Meta ads account that's been running for at least 12 months.

**🟡 Secondary ICPs:**

- **Pre-revenue or sub-$500K brands.** We turn most of these down — they're not big enough to benefit from us yet. We refer them to courses and lower-cost services.
- **$20M+ brands.** Usually have in-house growth teams. We don't pursue them but we don't reject them either if they come to us with a specific project.

## Psychographics

**🟢 The emotional state when they find us:**

They are tired and skeptical. They've already fired at least one agency. They've watched their ROAS climb in reports while their bank account doesn't grow. They've spent weekends inside Meta Ads Manager trying to figure out what their previous agency was actually doing. They are simultaneously hopeful that growth is possible and exhausted by the search for an agency that isn't going to disappoint them again.

They are also smart. They've read the books. They know what LTV and CAC mean. They are not novices. They want to talk to someone who knows MORE than they do, not less.

**🟢 Top 3 pains in their own words:**

1. *"My ROAS looks fine in the dashboard but my bank account isn't growing."*
2. *"My last agency just ran the same five ads to the same audiences for six months and called it strategy."*
3. *"I know I'm leaving money on the table with email but I have no idea how to fix it without hiring three more people."*

**🟢 Top 3 desires in their own words:**

1. *"I want to know my actual contribution margin after ad spend, not just my ROAS."*
2. *"I want an agency that talks to me like another founder, not like a vendor."*
3. *"I want to grow without watching my margins disappear."*

**🟢 What's keeping them up at night:**
The slow realization that they might be running a vanity business — high revenue, low profit, no path to becoming the company they wanted to build. The fear of hiring another expensive agency that wastes another 6 months.

## Beliefs

**🟢 What they currently believe about growth agencies:**

- That most agencies don't actually understand DTC e-commerce specifically.
- That account managers cycle through quickly and they'll end up explaining their business 4 times in 12 months.
- That ROAS is the metric agencies optimize for, even though it's not the metric founders care about.
- That creative is the bottleneck most agencies hide behind ("the creative isn't working" is the universal excuse).

**🟢 What they need to believe to buy:**

- That margin matters more than ROAS, and an agency that doesn't talk about margin isn't worth hiring.
- That a month-to-month contract is actually possible. (Most have been burned by 6-month minimums.)
- That a smaller, specialized agency can outperform a bigger generalist one.
- That the founder running the agency is the one who'll actually be paying attention to their account.

**🟢 Where they've been burned before:**

- Hired a name-brand agency for $15K/month, got 6 months of "we're still in the testing phase."
- Hired a freelancer for $2K/month who disappeared after two weeks.
- Tried to do it themselves, burned out, hired an agency, fired the agency, tried again.
- Got sold on "creative is everything" by an agency that delivered 30 generic UGC ads and zero growth.

**🟡 Common objections + how we respond:**

- *"You're more expensive than my last agency."* → "We're priced to be profitable for a $1M-$20M brand. If we cost less, we'd have to sacrifice the team's attention, which is the thing you're actually paying for."
- *"Why month-to-month? That feels risky for you."* → "It's risky if we're not delivering. We'd rather earn the relationship every month than lock you in."
- *"What if your strategist quits?"* → "Sarah is in every quarterly review and reads every monthly report. Even if your strategist changes, the relationship with the agency doesn't reset."
- *"Can you guarantee ROAS targets?"* → "No. Any agency that does is either lying or about to under-deliver. We guarantee process and transparency, not numbers we don't control."

## Vocabulary

**🟢 Words and phrases they use (mirror these in copy):**

- "Contribution margin," "blended CAC," "incremental ROAS," "LTV:CAC ratio"
- "Net new vs. retained revenue," "first-purchase economics"
- "Klaviyo flows," "abandoned cart sequences," "post-purchase series"
- "Creative fatigue," "audience saturation"
- "MMM" (marketing mix modeling) for the more advanced ones
- "Founder-led," "lean team," "scrappy"
- "Iterate," "test, learn, double down"

**🟢 Words they roll their eyes at (NEVER use):**

- "Synergy," "leverage" (as a verb), "ecosystem"
- "World-class," "best-in-class," "premium" without proof
- "ROAS" used as the headline success metric
- "Brand awareness" without a measurable outcome attached
- "Transformative results," "game-changing campaigns"
- "Our proprietary methodology" (every agency claims this)
- "Hashtag growth hacking"

**🟡 Inside references this audience gets:**

- Common Thread, Pilothouse, Disruptor School (the big agency names)
- Triple Whale, North Beam, Polar Analytics (the attribution platforms)
- Klaviyo, Postscript, Recharge (the email/SMS/subscription stack)
- The phrase "ROAS doesn't pay the bills, contribution margin does"

## Behavior

**🟡 Where they spend their attention:**

- **Content platforms:** Twitter/X for industry commentary, LinkedIn for tactical content, YouTube for long-form interviews with other founders
- **Communities:** Pilothouse podcast, Operators podcast, specific Slack communities, founder masterminds
- **Decision-influencers:** Other DTC founders they personally know, Nik Sharma, the Triple Whale team, specific podcast hosts

**🟡 How they make buying decisions:**

A slow, deliberate process. They will spend 2-6 weeks evaluating an agency. They'll ask for case studies, references, sample reporting. They'll ask to talk to a current client. They'll negotiate. They are NOT impulse buyers. The sales cycle is 3-8 weeks.

## Hero Customer (Example of ICP)

**🟡 A specific named example:**

Maya Chen, founder of a 7-figure DTC skincare brand. Inherited the brand from her family, modernized it, hit $4M in revenue in 3 years. Hired Westwood after firing two previous agencies. Came in with a 1.8 ROAS and a contribution margin she didn't even know how to calculate. Maya's quote: *"I stopped feeling like I was the smartest person in the room about my own marketing. Finally."*

## How to Use This Document

When drafting marketing, sales, or content for Westwood Marketing, the AI should:

1. Write FOR this specific founder, not a generic e-commerce audience.
2. Use their vocabulary (contribution margin, blended CAC, Klaviyo flows). Avoid the eye-roll list.
3. Address their actual pain (ROAS theater, agency disappointment) before pitching anything.
4. Acknowledge their past agency disappointments explicitly. Pretend they haven't been burned and lose them.
5. Lead with math, never with motivation.
