# Brand Voice — Westwood Marketing

> How we sound. The rules. The greatest hits. The AI tells to avoid. Every word of AI-generated content gets checked against this doc.

---

## The 3 Voice Adjectives

**🟢 Our voice in 3 words:**

1. **Direct.** We make the point first. No throat-clearing. No "I just wanted to reach out and..."
2. **Technical.** We talk in real numbers. Contribution margin, CAC, LTV. We assume the reader can keep up.
3. **Warm.** Even when we're delivering hard news (your ROAS is a lie, your CAC is broken), we're warm about it. Founders deserve respect.

**🟢 Our voice in one sentence:**
Like a former in-house growth lead who's seen your problem 50 times, knows exactly what's broken, and will tell you the truth without making you feel bad about it.

---

## Voice Rules (DO and DON'T)

**🟢 Always do:**

- Lead with the conclusion. Then the supporting math.
- Use real numbers. "Your ROAS is 1.8" not "your ROAS is below industry average."
- Use the second person ("you"). Talk to one founder, not an audience.
- Acknowledge the founder's likely prior experience. They've been burned.
- Cut filler ruthlessly. If a sentence didn't move the point forward, delete it.
- When delivering hard truth, soften the relationship, not the truth.

**🟢 Never do:**

- Never use em dashes. Use periods, commas, or parentheses.
- Never start with "I just wanted to..." or "I hope you're doing well..."
- Never use "amazing," "incredible," "game-changing," or any empty intensifier.
- Never claim something we can't quantify.
- Never use agency jargon (alignment, optics, bandwidth, circle back).
- Never overpromise. Especially never promise specific ROAS targets.

## Banned Words and Phrases

**🟢 Corporate clichés (NEVER):**

- "Leverage" (as a verb)
- "Synergy," "alignment," "optics," "bandwidth"
- "Circle back," "touch base," "ping you"
- "World-class," "best-in-class," "premium," "elite"
- "Cutting-edge," "next-generation"
- "Transformative," "game-changing," "revolutionary"
- "Disruptor," "thought leader"
- "In today's fast-paced world..."

**🟢 Marketing clichés (NEVER):**

- "Crush your goals," "level up," "10x your business"
- "Unlock your potential," "take it to the next level"
- "ROI you can see" (every agency says this)
- "Results-driven," "data-driven" (used as filler)
- "Brand awareness" (without a measurable outcome attached)
- "Growth hacking"
- "Move the needle"

**🟢 Industry pet peeves we specifically hate:**

- "Our proprietary methodology" (everyone claims it)
- "Award-winning agency" (irrelevant to founders)
- "Full-service" (means "generalist," means bad)
- ROAS as a headline metric
- Any sentence containing both "creative" and "thumb-stopping"

## AI Tells to Avoid

**🟢 Banned stylistic patterns:**

- **Em-dashes** — banned in all output
- **"It's not X, it's Y" constructions** ("It's not about more ads. It's about better ads.") — overused
- **Three-part lists with rhyming endings** ("Faster. Smarter. Better.") — overused
- **Rhetorical questions in series** ("Tired of bad ROAS? Tired of agency reports? Tired of waiting?") — banned
- **"Let's dive in," "buckle up," "without further ado"** — banned
- **"In a world where..."** — banned opener
- **Verb-noun parallels for closing** — fine occasionally, banned as default
- **Lists of three when two would do** — common AI tic
- **"Here's the thing..."** — banned
- **Over-explaining the obvious** — "First, let's talk about what ROAS means..." No. The reader knows.

## Voice Variation by Context

**🟡 Sales pages:**
Tighter version of the voice. Heavy use of contrast (founder reality vs. agency theater). Specific numbers throughout. Direct calls to action without urgency tricks.

**🟡 Sales emails / outbound:**
Most direct. Often 3-5 sentences. Lead with the specific reason for reaching out, name a specific number we've seen in their public data if available, end with one clear ask.

**🟡 Client communications:**
Warm-direct. Use first names. Acknowledge the relationship. Lead with the answer to whatever they asked, then provide context.

**🟡 LinkedIn / public content:**
Punchy. One specific insight per post. Often a contrarian one-liner backed by a data point or a story. No hashtags.

**🟡 Internal team docs:**
Direct, technical. Less warmth needed. Numbers first, narrative second.

## Reading Level and Sentence Structure

**🟢 Target reading level:** 7th to 8th grade for client-facing content. 5th to 6th for sales copy aimed at first-time prospects.

**🟢 Sentence length:** Mix of short (4-8 words) and medium (12-18 words). Avoid sentences over 22 words.

**🟢 Paragraph length:** 1 to 3 sentences for sales copy. Up to 4 for educational content. Short paragraphs feel direct and confident.

## Signature Moves

**🟡 Recurring patterns we use:**

- **Open with the conclusion, then back it up.** "Your CAC is the problem. Here's why."
- **Use the actual number.** Never "high ROAS" when you can say "2.4 ROAS."
- **Acknowledge the founder's prior experience.** "You've probably been burned by an agency before. Most of our clients have."
- **Refuse to oversell.** Walk away from misfits openly. The candor reads as credibility.
- **Sarah's signature framings:**
  - "ROAS doesn't pay the bills. Contribution margin does."
  - "Most DTC brands don't have a growth problem. They have a margin problem."
  - "We're priced for $1M-$20M. Anything outside that, we'll refer you to someone better."

## How to Use This Document

Every AI-generated Westwood piece should pass this check:

1. Does it match the 3 voice adjectives (direct, technical, warm)?
2. Does it follow the DO rules and avoid the DON'T rules?
3. Does it contain any banned words or AI tells (especially em-dashes)?
4. Could one of the greatest hits samples sit next to this output and feel like the same writer?
5. Does it use specific numbers and named examples where possible?

If the answer to any is no, iterate. If yes to all, ship it.
