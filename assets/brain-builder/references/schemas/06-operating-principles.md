# Schema: 06 Operating Principles

This is the markdown structure for the Operating Principles document. How decisions get made, how work moves, what standards apply. The hardest doc to draft — almost entirely interview-driven.

## Output filename
`06_Operating_Principles.md`

## Required structure

```markdown
# Operating Principles — [BUSINESS NAME]

> How we work. How we decide. What we say YES to and what we say NO to. The standards and rhythms that govern this business. An AI loaded with this Brain should make decisions consistent with this doc.

---

## Decision-Making Principles

**🟢 The 3 to 5 lenses we use when stuck on a hard call:**

For each, format as:

- **[Principle name]:** [One paragraph. What's the principle, what does it look like in practice, when does it apply.]

Examples to inspire (not to copy):
- "Customer first, always." — When two paths are possible, we pick the one that's better for the customer even when it costs us short-term revenue.
- "Speed over polish." — We ship at 80% rather than polishing to 100%. Done is better than perfect.
- "Simple over clever." — Given two solutions, we pick the one that's easier to explain.

---

## Work Ownership Map (The 4Ds)

> Where each function in the business currently sits across the 4Ds, and where it's heading. This section gives every AI employee a strategic view of work ownership in the business.

**🟢 The 4Ds (for reference):**

- **Doing** — You execute the work yourself
- **Deciding** — You don't execute but every instance needs your judgment
- **Delegating** — A team member or AI employee owns the work, runs without you
- **Designing** — You're out of the work, architecting the system

| Sales | [Name] | [Mode] | [Mode] | [Notes] |
| Product / Delivery | [Name] | [Mode] | [Mode] | [Notes] |
| Customer Success | [Name] | [Mode] | [Mode] | [Notes] |
| Operations | [Name] | [Mode] | [Mode] | [Notes] |
| Finance | [Name] | [Mode] | [Mode] | [Notes] |
| [Custom function 1] | [Name] | [Mode] | [Mode] | [Notes] |

**🟢 Functions where the founder stays at Designing permanently:**

[List any functions where the founder/owner intends to remain in the Designing mode indefinitely — strategic, architecture-level work that requires their judgment. Usually: strategic direction, hiring senior team, major partnerships, brand voice approval. These are NOT candidates for AI delegation by design.]

## How to Use This Document

When the AI is making decisions on behalf of this business (drafting responses, evaluating offers, handling edge cases), it should:
1. Check the Decision-Making Principles for the relevant lens.
2. Match communication norms based on context.
3. Hit the quality bar for that content type.
4. Never violate the "What We Say NO To" list.
5. Default to the customer service philosophy when handling problems.
6. When in doubt, escalate to the founder rather than guess.
```

## Drafting notes

- Operating Principles is almost entirely interview-driven. Expect to mark many sections `[TO REFINE]` after a first draft.
- Specificity matters even more here than in other docs. "We value our customers" is useless. "We refund without question if they ask within 60 days" is useful.
- Mine sales call transcripts and customer service emails for actual operating norms. The way the user handles tough situations IS the operating principle.
- The "What We Say YES/NO To" lists are often the most distinctive sections. Push the user for specifics.
- Mark `[TO REFINE]` aggressively. Better to ship with gaps than ship with generic placeholder content.
