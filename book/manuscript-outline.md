# I PUT MYSELF ON A LEARNING PLAN
### How Minority Women Can Learn New Technology, Build Proof, and Design Their Own Access

**Status: CONFIRMED FINAL OUTLINE.** This is the canonical structure. Do not restart the strategy process.

**Core promise:** This book helps minority women stop confusing exclusion with inability, build their own learning plan for new technology, create proof, and become access for the next woman.

**Core belief:** You do not have to know it yet. You need to know how to learn it.

**Length target:** 12 chapters + Introduction + Conclusion. 2,500–3,000 words per chapter.

---

## INTRODUCTION — They Put Me on a Performance Plan. I Put Myself on a Learning Plan.

**Opening story:** Orange County Government. Asked to learn higher technology. Told to go back to school on my own time. Learned from the interns anyway. Completed the project. Then my growth became a problem.

**Main lesson:** Sometimes the problem is not your ability. Sometimes the problem is who controls access.

**Key quote:** *I was not having a performance problem. I was having an access problem.*

**Reader takeaway:** This book is not about becoming fearless overnight. It is about learning what to do when fear, confusion, exclusion, or age tries to convince you that technology is not for you.

---

# PART ONE — The Room Was Not Built for How I Learned

## CHAPTER 1 — The Only Woman in the Room
**Opening story:** In the technology program surrounded by men. The only woman in the room. The technology is being explained, but not in a way my brain receives.
**Main lesson:** Not understanding the way something is taught does not mean you cannot learn it.
**Reader exercise:** Write down one time you thought, "Maybe I'm not good at this." Then ask: Was I incapable, or was it not taught in a way I could receive?
**Key quote:** *Do not mistake exclusion for inability.*

## CHAPTER 2 — Lipstick and Logic Can Live in the Same Mind
**Opening story:** Fashion-conscious and tech-conscious. People assume glam and intelligence cannot sit in the same woman.
**Main lesson:** Your creativity is not separate from your technical ability. It may be one of the reasons you learn technology differently.
**Reader exercise:** List three creative strengths you already have. Then write how each one could help you learn or use technology.
**Key quote:** *I did not have to take off my creativity to put on my intelligence.*

---

# PART TWO — I Learned Through Correction, Not Shame

## CHAPTER 3 — The D Was Data
**Opening story:** Calculus. Struggling. Getting a D, but loving solving the problems.
**Main lesson:** A bad result tells you where you are. It does not tell you who you are.
**Reader exercise:** Pick one technology skill you feel weak in. Write: "My current result is data. It is not my identity."
**Key quote:** *The D told me where I was. It did not tell me who I was.*

## CHAPTER 4 — Look Beside You
**Opening story:** The student with the thick glasses is getting A's. I ask him for help.
**Main lesson:** The person who can help you may not be famous. Look for evidence, not status.
**Reader exercise:** Identify one person, class, group, mentor, or tool that can help you understand your next technology goal.
**Key quote:** *He knew something I needed to understand.*

## CHAPTER 5 — Do the Homework First
**Opening story:** He tells me, "Go home, do your homework, and then I'll help you." I do the work first, then bring it back for correction.
**Main lesson:** Community is not rescue. Real learning requires effort before correction.
**Reader exercise:** Before asking for help, write: What did I try? Where did I get stuck? What result did I get?
**Key quote:** *Correction works better when effort is already on the table.*

---

# PART THREE — Access Was in the Building

## CHAPTER 6 — The Limit I Was Assigned
**Opening story:** Six months into the job, I ask to learn more. Told to go back to school. Later, after a strong performance review, I ask again.
**Main lesson:** Sometimes people do not block you because you cannot learn. They block you because they have assigned you a limit.
**Reader exercise:** Write down one limit someone assigned to you. Then write the evidence that proves that limit is not final.
**Key quote:** *I was not asking for special treatment. I was asking for access to growth.*

## CHAPTER 7 — When My Performance Couldn't Be Questioned
**Opening story:** I learn from the interns. Someone trusts me with the project. I do the work well. Then my supervisor finds out and becomes angry.
**Main lesson:** When the work proves you can grow, some people may shift the focus from your performance to your personality.
**Reader exercise:** Write down the facts before accepting someone's label. What happened? What did you complete? What was the result? Who saw the work?
**Key quote:** *When my performance could not be questioned, my personality was.*

## CHAPTER 8 — My Curiosity Became a Case File
**Opening story:** Called a drama queen. Told I am unstable. Told to leave the building. Then the psychologist recommendation and release request appear. I read the HR manual and call HR.
**Main lesson:** When someone labels you, learn the system. Do not surrender your rights, your records, or your identity to someone else's narrative.
**Reader exercise:** Before signing anything, ask: What am I giving access to? Who has authority? What policy applies? What documentation exists?
**Key quote:** *She tried to make my ambition look like instability.*

---

# PART FOUR — Technology Belongs to the Builder

## CHAPTER 9 — I Learned to Test the System
**Opening story:** Robotics. Testing. Troubleshooting. Looking at what was supposed to happen, what actually happened, and where the process broke.
**Main lesson:** Technology is less scary when you learn to test it instead of worship it.
**Reader exercise:** Pick one tool. Ask: What did I expect it to do? What did it actually do? Where did it break? What can I change and try again?
**Key quote:** *A breakdown is not the end. It is the beginning of the test.*

## CHAPTER 10 — One Small Win Beats Twenty Saved Tutorials
**Opening story:** The shift from watching technology to building with it. AI, web, automation, robotics, business systems — the pattern is the same.
**Main lesson:** You do not build confidence by collecting information. You build confidence by creating proof.
**Reader exercise:** Choose one small technology project and finish it in seven days. Examples: a better bio, a client follow-up message, a simple AI prompt, a workflow checklist, a business service description, a child's study guide, a small automation plan.
**Key quote:** *Proof gives confidence somewhere to stand.*

---

# PART FIVE — I Designed Another Building

## CHAPTER 11 — The Table Was Still Inside Their Building
**Opening story:** The old saying says, "Build your own table." But the table is still inside someone else's building.
**Main lesson:** You do not only want a seat. You want to design a place where women can learn, build, rise, and receive keys.
**Reader exercise:** Describe the building you would design. Who gets access? What is taught on each floor? How do people move up? Who gets a key?
**Key quote:** *The table was still inside their building. I wanted more than a chair.*

## CHAPTER 12 — Become Somebody Else's Access
**Opening story:** Teaching underserved youth. Computers, entrepreneurship, robotics, and technology. Training young people to come back and teach others.
**Main lesson:** The highest use of learning is not just personal success. It is opening access for someone else.
**Reader exercise:** Name one woman, child, client, friend, church member, or community member you can help with something you already know.
**Key quote:** *Learn it. Then become somebody else's access.*

---

## CONCLUSION — Put Yourself on the Next Learning Plan

**Closing message:** AI will change. Technology will change. Jobs will change. The tools will change. But the woman who knows how to learn is never helpless.

**Final reader action:** Create a 30-day learning plan for one technology skill.

**Final quote:** *You do not have to know it yet. You need to know how to learn it.*

## NEW MATERIAL TO PLACE — THE EEOC / FIRE DEPARTMENT BACKSTORY

Added after outline confirmation. **Does not belong in Chapter 1** (Chapter 1 is the school technology program). This is Orange County Government material and belongs in the **Introduction and Part Three**.

**The facts as given:**
- I was working in the fire department. My title was a technical title.
- The county moved all the technical positions into the technical department — and left mine sitting in the fire department. Mine should have moved too. It did not.
- Once I was left behind, they tried to turn me into a glorified secretary. A technical title doing clerical work.
- I filed two EEOC complaints.
- After I filed, they moved me to Information Systems and Services — which absorbed my technical title into the technical department, which is exactly what should have happened in the first place.

**Why this matters to the book:** This is the cleanest, hardest piece of evidence in the entire manuscript that the problem was **access, not ability**. The correct placement of my title was never in question. It was always technical. They simply did not move it — and then filled the gap with clerical work. It took a federal complaint to get the thing that should have happened automatically.

**Placement plan:**
- **Introduction:** Add as the setup before "I asked to learn higher technology." It establishes that I did not arrive at that job asking for a favor. I arrived having already had to fight to be filed correctly. Strengthens *I was not having a performance problem. I was having an access problem.*
- **Chapter 6 (The Limit I Was Assigned):** This is the strongest home for the full scene. The limit was assigned before a single word was said about my performance — it was assigned by a filing decision. Being made a glorified secretary is the limit, made visible.
- **Chapter 8 (My Curiosity Became a Case File):** Connect the two moments. I had already read a system once and used it correctly. So when the psychologist recommendation and the release request appeared, reading the HR manual and calling HR was not new behavior for me. It was the second time. That is a pattern, not a reaction.

**New candidate key lines from this material:**
- *They moved everybody's title where it belonged. They left mine.*
- *A technical title doing clerical work is not a promotion problem. It is a placement problem.*
- *It took a federal complaint to get me filed correctly.*

**Title/role detail added:** the technical title in question was **Website Support and Development**. Confirm exact wording before it goes into the Introduction and Chapter 6 — this is the title that should have moved with the other technical positions and did not. Naming it specifically makes the "glorified secretary" contrast land harder: a Website Support and Development title, doing clerical work, sitting in the fire department.

## NEW MATERIAL TO PLACE — THE $48,000, THE ADVICE, AND THE REGRET

Added after outline confirmation. This is Part Three and Conclusion material.

**The facts as given:**
- This was a long time ago. The salary was $48,000.
- They did not really want me to do much work. They were going to pay me to sit down and be quiet.
- My ex-husband told me to roll with the punches, take the salary, and challenge myself somewhere else.
- Looking back now, I think maybe I could have kept the role, kept doing what I was doing, kept learning from the interns without taking on major projects — and used that stability to restart my youth program, the one I had going when I received funding from Eli Lilly.
- Looking back, I think that is what I should have done.

**Why this matters to the book:** This is the most honest thing in the manuscript, and it is the thing that will make readers trust every other page. Most books in this category end on pure triumph. This does not. This says: I was right about what they did to me, *and* I have thought hard about what I would do differently — and neither one of those cancels the other out. That is a grown woman talking. That is what separates this book from a motivational book.

It also reframes the whole idea of "being paid to be quiet." A salary offered in exchange for your silence and your stillness is not a job. It is a purchase. And a woman is allowed to look at that purchase and make a strategic decision about it — including the decision to take the check and spend the energy somewhere it actually compounds.

**Placement plan:**
- **Chapter 6 (The Limit I Was Assigned):** The $48,000 goes here, stated plainly. Name the number. The number is the receipt. They were not underpaying me — they were paying me *to not grow*. That is what the money was actually for. New contrast: *A salary is not always payment for your work. Sometimes it is payment for your silence.*
- **Chapter 8 (My Curiosity Became a Case File):** The ex-husband's advice belongs here, in the middle of the fight. "Roll with the punches" is what the people who love us say when they are trying to protect us from a cost they can see and we cannot. Do not make him a villain. He was not wrong about the cost. He was wrong about what it would cost me to stay quiet.
- **Conclusion (Put Yourself on the Next Learning Plan):** The retrospect. This is the strongest possible close before the 30-day plan, because it is the one place the reader is taught something the author paid full price for. Teach the actual lesson underneath it: **the fight is not always the plan.** Sometimes the smartest move is to keep the check, keep learning quietly from whoever has the access, and pour the real energy into the thing you own. Frame it as strategy, not surrender — a woman deciding where her energy compounds.

**The strategic teaching this unlocks (belongs in the Conclusion):**
Three options exist any time a room limits you. Most women only get told about two.
1. Stay and fight the room.
2. Leave the room.
3. Stay, take the check, take the learning, give the room the minimum it is paying for — and build your own thing on the side with the energy you saved.
Option three is not cowardice. Option three is a plan. It is the only one of the three where you keep both the money and the momentum.

**The Eli Lilly youth program:** This is the first real evidence in her life that she could build her own building — she had already been funded to do it once, before any of the county mess. Move a reference to it *earlier* than Chapter 12 so it does not read as an afterthought. Plant it in Chapter 6 or 8 as the thing she already had in her back pocket the entire time she was fighting for a floor in somebody else's building. Then Chapter 12 pays it off.

**New candidate key lines from this material:**
- *A salary is not always payment for your work. Sometimes it is payment for your silence.*
- *They were not paying me $48,000 to work. They were paying me $48,000 to sit down.*
- *He told me to roll with the punches. He was not wrong about the cost. He was wrong about which one was cheaper.*
- *The fight is not always the plan. Sometimes the plan is where you spend the energy the fight would have cost you.*
- *I already had a building. I was standing outside somebody else's, asking to be let up a floor.*
